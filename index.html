FAQ

Why is FinDex free?

FinDex is the first evidence-based reporting database built for FinTech, and a reliable record has to be built from the start. Free access invites verified organizations to contribute verifiable incidents, not attestations. Checking and disputing your own record will always be free. As the database grows, so will the cost of maintaining it. Any future pricing will be announced well in advance, with affordability and accessibility remaining priorities.

Who can search for other parties on FinDex?

Verified organizations in FinTech (brokers, funders, lenders, software and lead providers) with a permissible purpose: Employment screening, partner diligence, underwriting. One account per company.

Can I see what’s been reported about me or my business?

Yes. Create a free account and verify your identity. You’ll only see incidents about you or your business.

Will I be told if I’m reported?

Yes. Companies must provide your email address when they report an incident. You’ll receive an email naming the company that submitted the report, and you’ll have 10 days to respond before it’s published.

How do I dispute a report?

Create an account by selecting "Check My Record" and dispute it at no cost. Disputed reports are withheld while we review the evidence, and inaccurate or unverifiable information is corrected or removed.

What can be reported?
  • Individuals (employees and contractors): data theft or backdooring, document fraud, impersonation, and intentionally deceiving merchants.
  • Partners: data theft or backdooring, impersonation, mishandling of your data, and intentionally deceiving merchants or partners.
  • Funded businesses: document fraud, impersonation (such as identity theft to obtain funding), payment delinquency (one or more missed payments, reported once as a single event), and defaults. Only funders and lenders can report payment delinquencies and defaults.
What counts as evidence?

Evidence must be documented and verifiable. Attestations are not accepted on their own. Every submission undergoes an independent and human review before publication. Submission of evidence does not guarantee publication. Examples of evidence include:

  • Backdooring & Data Theft: Evidence showing company or merchant information was sent outside your organization, such as an email with attachments sent to an external address, file transfer records, or export logs, with an explanation of what was shared, where it was sent, and why you believe the reported party was responsible. Do not submit the sensitive data itself, only evidence of its unauthorized disclosure.
  • Deception: The misleading statement or communication and who received it, with an explanation of why you believe the reported party was responsible. Emails, messages, or recorded phone calls may be submitted. For phone calls, provide the call log and recording or transcript exported directly from your phone system (not a manually typed document), plus evidence linking the reported party to their phone number and the recipient’s number to the merchant or business, such as user profiles, CRM records, or directory listings. Calls must have been lawfully recorded.
  • Document Fraud: Evidence showing what was altered or falsified and linking it to the reported party, with an explanation of why you believe they are responsible. Email correspondence, fraud-detection results, or original and altered versions of the document may be submitted as supporting evidence.
  • Impersonation: Communication or activity showing someone falsely representing themselves as another person or company, with an explanation of who was impersonated, how it occurred, and why you believe the reported party is responsible. Emails, messages, call recordings, or phone system records may be submitted as supporting evidence.
  • Partner Data Mishandling: Evidence showing your data was accessed, shared, exposed, or used without authorization, with an explanation of how the partner or vendor mishandled it and why you believe they were responsible. Emails, messages, breach notifications, access logs, or recorded phone calls may be submitted. Do not submit the sensitive data itself.
  • Repayment Issues: A payment delinquency is one or more missed or returned payments, reported at the discretion of the funder or lender, once as a single event rather than each missed payment. A screenshot from your payment system showing the missed or returned payments and the past-due amount is required, and the reporter keeps the status current (delinquent or caught up). This helps ensure reports are based on actual repayment records rather than unsubstantiated claims. For defaults, a copy of the formal default notice and proof that it was sent to the merchant are also required.
What do searchers see?

Published reports only: the incident date, category, reporting company, description, and evidence. Date of birth, SSN, and EIN are never shown.

What happens if a company submits a false report?

Companies must certify that every report is accurate and supported by evidence. Reports found to contain false or falsified information are removed. Companies that repeatedly submit falsified reports may lose access to FinDex and become subject to an evidence-based misconduct report themselves.

Can I report incidents from the past?

Yes. Misconduct incidents, including data theft, fraud, impersonation, or deception, may be reported up to 7 years after they occurred. Repayment issues must be reported within 90 days of the incident to help establish a reliable database built on recent, verifiable payment activity. All reports must be supported by evidence, regardless of when the incident occurred.

How long are records kept?

Seven years. After that, records and evidence are permanently deleted (unless actively in dispute).

Is my information secure?

Identity details such as date of birth, SSN, and EIN are encrypted and masked everywhere they appear, and never included in emails. Evidence is kept on SOC 2 Type II infrastructure. Our reviewers may use AI tools to summarize reporting; every publication decision is made by a person.

How does FinDex comply with the FCRA?
  • Permissible purpose: Every search records its purpose. Employment screening requires the employer’s certification and the individual’s signed authorization.
  • Accuracy: Evidence is independently reviewed before publication, and unverifiable information is not published.
  • Notice: Reported parties are notified, with the reporting company named and the CFPB Summary of Rights included, 10 days before publication.
  • Disputes: Disputed reports are withheld during review and reinvestigated within 30 days.
  • Access logs: Every search is logged (who, when, and why), and you can see who accessed your record in your account.
  • Member obligations: Participating organizations receive the CFPB Notice to Users and Notice to Furnishers.
  • Your own record: Viewing, disputing, and appealing your record is always free.
What does “independently administered by Aquamark” mean?

Aquamark is responsible for operating FinDex, establishing reporting standards, reviewing submitted evidence, verifying participating organizations, and overseeing the accuracy and integrity of records.

FinDex operates under its own policies and procedures, with reporting decisions based on documented evidence rather than unverified allegations or attestations.
