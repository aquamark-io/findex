Privacy Policy

1. Who we are

FinDex is an evidence-based reporting database for the FinTech industry. It is operated by Duncan Consulting LLC, doing business as FinDex (“FinDex,” “we,” “us”), based in San Francisco, California. FinDex is independently administered by Aquamark, which operates the service, sets reporting standards, verifies participating organizations, and reviews submitted evidence. Counsel to confirm how the relationship between Duncan Consulting LLC and Aquamark should be described, and which entity is the consumer reporting agency.

This policy covers our website at joinfindex.com and the FinDex portal at app.joinfindex.com. It explains what information we collect, where it comes from, how we use and share it, how long we keep it, how we protect it, and the choices and rights you have.

2. What FinDex reports are, and what they are not

A FinDex report is a record that a verified organization (a “reporting member”) submitted about a specific incident, together with the documents it provided to support it. Before a report is published, our reviewers check that the evidence supports the specific statement that will be published, not only that something happened. For example, records showing that files were downloaded do not, on their own, support a statement that the files were sent outside the company.

  • Reports describe documented conduct. Each report shows the incident date, the category, the reporting company, a description, and the evidence. Categories describe what the evidence shows happened. They are not legal conclusions.
  • Reports are not findings of guilt. FinDex does not decide whether anyone committed a crime or is legally liable, and a published report does not mean a court, regulator, or law enforcement agency has made any finding.
  • FinDex does not collect criminal records. We do not collect or report arrest records, criminal charges, court records, or convictions, and reporting members may not submit them as evidence. Counsel to confirm this policy and the handling of evidence that refers to a police report or pending case.
  • FinDex does not score or recommend. We do not rate, score, rank, or recommend for or against any person or business, and we do not tell members whether to hire, partner with, or fund anyone. Each member makes its own decisions.
  • The absence of a report means nothing. A search with no results does not mean a person or business has no history.

3. FinDex and the Fair Credit Reporting Act

Reports about individuals that are used for decisions such as employment may be “consumer reports” under the Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA), and reports about a person’s character or conduct may also be “investigative consumer reports” under federal and state law. FinDex applies the FCRA’s protections to reports about individuals. Reports about businesses generally fall outside the FCRA, but FinDex applies the same notice, access, and dispute process to everyone. Counsel to confirm how FinDex is characterized under the FCRA and California law, which reports are consumer reports or investigative consumer reports, and how reports about sole proprietors and personally guaranteed businesses are treated.

In practice, this means:

  • Only verified organizations can search, and every search must state a permissible purpose: employment screening, partner diligence, or underwriting. Employment screening also requires the employer’s certification and the individual’s signed authorization.
  • Every report must be supported by evidence and is independently reviewed before anything is published.
  • Reported parties are notified by email, with the reporting company named, at least 10 days before a report is published. Individuals also receive the CFPB’s A Summary of Your Rights Under the Fair Credit Reporting Act.
  • Anyone can view and dispute what has been reported about them, at no cost.
  • Reports are limited to incidents within the past 7 years.

4. Information we collect, and where it comes from

Member organizations

When a company requests access, we collect the requester’s name, business email address, job title, and phone number, and the company’s legal name, DBA, website, company type, and state of registration. We also record acceptance of the Terms of Use, the indemnification terms, and acknowledgment of the CFPB Notice to Users and Notice to Furnishers, with the version and time accepted.

People and businesses checking their own record

  • Individuals: name, email address, date of birth, the last four digits of their Social Security number, current or most recent company, job title, and type of work.
  • Companies and funded businesses: the contact’s name and email address, and the business’s legal name, DBA, website, and EIN (or, for a business without an EIN, the owner’s date of birth and the last four digits of their Social Security number).

We use these details to verify identity and to match a person or business to reports about them.

Reports and evidence

Reports come from reporting members, not from the people or businesses they are about. When a member submits a report, we collect information about the reported party (such as name, email address, contact person, website, date of birth, last four digits of a Social Security number, or EIN), a description of the incident, its date, and supporting evidence files. For defaults, we also collect the default notice and proof that it was sent. Reporters must redact full Social Security and bank account numbers from evidence before submitting it.

Evidence can contain information about other people, such as the recipient of an email or a merchant on a recorded call. Reporters must limit evidence to what is needed to support the report. Counsel to confirm obligations to people who appear in evidence but are not the reported party.

Disputes

When a reported party disputes a report, we collect their explanation and any files they provide, and the reporting company’s response and files.

Searches

When a member searches, we record the identifiers entered, the stated permissible purpose, the certification, any employment authorization form, who searched and when, and which reports were shown. Dates of birth, Social Security numbers, and EINs entered in a search are not stored in the search log.

Technical information

We record IP addresses and timestamps for account and report activity, email delivery events (such as bounced notices), and the cookies described in section 8.

5. How we use information

  • To verify organizations and individuals and operate accounts, including passwordless sign-in.
  • To review reports and evidence, notify reported parties, publish verified reports, and handle disputes.
  • To return search results to members with a permissible purpose, and to keep the records the FCRA requires, including who accessed a record and why.
  • To secure the service, prevent abuse, and investigate falsified reports.
  • To send service emails, such as sign-in codes, notices, and dispute updates.
  • To comply with law and enforce our Terms of Use.

We do not sell personal information, share it for cross-context behavioral advertising, or use it for advertising.

6. How information is shared

  • With members who search: published reports only, for the permissible purpose they certified, showing the incident date, category, reporting company, description, and evidence. Date of birth, Social Security numbers, and EINs are never shown. Results stay viewable for 7 days.
  • With reported parties: the notice names the reporting company and describes the report. Reported parties can view the report and its evidence, and can see which organizations accessed their record and for what purpose.
  • With reporting companies: the reported party’s dispute, explanation, and files about that company’s report, so it can respond.
  • With service providers who host and operate FinDex for us, under contracts that limit their use of the information.
  • When required by law, such as a subpoena or court order, or to protect the rights and safety of FinDex, our members, or others.
  • In a business transfer, such as a merger or acquisition, subject to this policy.

We do not provide reports to anyone for consumer credit, insurance, housing, or any purpose other than those listed in section 3.

7. AI-assisted evidence review

Our reviewers may use an AI tool to summarize evidence against what each report category requires. Evidence files and the report details are sent to our AI provider for this purpose, but only the last four digits of an SSN or EIN are ever shared. The summary is an aid for our reviewers: every decision to publish, reject, amend, or remove a report is made by a person. Counsel to confirm the AI provider’s data-use terms (for example, no training on submitted data) are described accurately, and whether any state law on automated decision-making applies.

8. Cookies

The portal uses only cookies that are strictly necessary to sign you in and keep you signed in: a session cookie that lasts up to 12 hours, and a short-lived cookie used during sign-in. These cookies are not readable by scripts on the page. We do not use analytics, advertising, or tracking cookies, and our website loads no third-party tracking scripts.

9. How long we keep information

  • Reports and evidence are permanently deleted 7 years after the incident date, unless the report is in an open dispute at that time.
  • Removed and rejected reports are kept on the same schedule as a record of what was submitted and decided, and are never shown in search results.
  • Search results can be viewed by the searching member for 7 days. Search and access logs, certifications, and employment authorization forms are kept for [retention period, as required by the FCRA and state law].
  • Dispute records are kept with the report they relate to. Confirm whether dispute records must be kept after the report is deleted.
  • Sign-in codes expire after 10 minutes and can be used once.
  • Account information is kept while the account is active and afterwards as long as needed to meet legal obligations. Confirm account retention period.

10. How we protect information

  • Encryption: all connections use HTTPS. Dates of birth, the last four digits of Social Security numbers, and EINs are encrypted in our database with AES-256-GCM, and are masked wherever they appear.
  • Never in emails or links: these identifiers are never included in any email, log, or web address.
  • Restricted database access: the database cannot be reached directly from the internet or by any public key. Only the FinDex server can read or write it.
  • Private evidence storage: evidence files are stored privately on SOC 2 Type II infrastructure. Each time an authorized person opens one, they get a link that expires after 2 minutes, and the access is logged.
  • Passwordless sign-in: members sign in with a one-time code sent to their email, so there are no passwords to steal or reuse. Sign-in attempts are rate-limited.
  • Audit trail: report decisions, evidence access, disputes, and searches are logged.
  • Minimal sharing: only the last four digits of an SSN or EIN are shared with our AI provider, and reporters must redact full Social Security and bank account numbers from evidence.

No system is perfectly secure. If we learn of a security incident affecting your information, we will notify you as required by law.

  • Add your side: Counsel to confirm whether individuals may add a brief statement to a disputed report that is kept, and how it is shown to searchers.
  • Adverse action: if an organization takes action against you based on a FinDex report, such as not hiring you, it must tell you, give you a copy of the report and a summary of your rights, and tell you how to contact FinDex. FinDex did not make that decision and cannot explain why it was made.
  • Update or delete your account: contact us. Some information may be kept where the law requires it, such as records of reports, disputes, and searches.

  • The Investigative Consumer Reporting Agencies Act (ICRAA), which covers reports about a person’s character, general reputation, or personal characteristics. This may include the right to receive a copy of any report obtained about you for employment, and to inspect the files FinDex holds about you. Counsel to confirm whether ICRAA applies and add its required disclosures, including the employer’s duty to offer a free copy of the report.
  • The Consumer Credit Reporting Agencies Act (CCRAA). Counsel to confirm whether it applies to any FinDex report.
  • The California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA). Information regulated by the FCRA is largely exempt from the CCPA, but other information (such as account, website, and member contact information) may not be. Counsel to confirm whether FinDex meets the CCPA thresholds and, if so, add the notice at collection, categories of personal information and sources, retention by category, and the rights to know, correct, delete, and limit use of sensitive personal information.
  • California Labor Code. Employers in California must follow state rules on background checks and on the use of criminal history in hiring. FinDex does not report criminal history.

Other states

Residents of other states may have additional rights, such as under New York’s Fair Credit Reporting Act or state consumer privacy laws. Counsel to confirm which state laws require added disclosures, and whether FinDex must register as a data broker in California, Vermont, Texas, Oregon, or elsewhere.

5. Reporting incidents

Members may report only incidents in the categories FinDex lists, about the party types each category allows, that occurred within the past 7 years. Only funders and lenders may report defaults. By submitting a report, you certify that:

  • It is accurate and complete. The report is true to the best of your knowledge after a reasonable investigation, is based on your organization’s own records and direct knowledge, and does not leave out facts that would change its meaning.
  • The evidence supports what you say. Your description states facts the evidence shows, not guesses, opinions, or legal conclusions (for example, “sent 40 merchant files to a personal email address,” not “stole our data”).
  • The evidence is genuine and lawfully obtained. Files are unaltered. Calls were recorded with any consent the law requires. You have the right to share the evidence with FinDex and with the reported party.
  • The evidence is limited to what is needed. Full Social Security and bank account numbers are redacted, and you do not submit the sensitive data itself, only evidence of what happened to it. Arrest records, criminal charges, court records, and convictions are not accepted.
